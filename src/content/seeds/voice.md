# The voice

The audience is writers and editors working on technical material. The assumption is that you write,
and that you have been edited. The entrance is [The hard parts](/writing/the-hard-parts).

## What is inside

- **Two essays** — on removing difficulty rather than hiding it, and on why boring machinery is the
  part that survives.
- **The rules, by example** — every page on this site is meant to be an instance of the voice, and
  the factors seed is the clearest demonstration: each lesson states a cost before a benefit.

## Why it is shaped this way

Technical prose usually fails in one of two directions: it asserts (and cannot be checked) or it
hedges (and says nothing). The voice here is a third option — state the trade, then the
recommendation, with a number or a named failure attached.

That is what "receipts instead of claims" means in practice:

- Not "robust publishing" but "the guard fails when the built asset paths disagree with the mount".
- Not "easy to maintain" but "five files, and here is the list".
- Not "powerful" but the exact command, and what it refuses.

The house joke is the same discipline applied to the titles: the legume idioms are used as ordinary
English and never explained. A reader who notices keeps it to themselves; a reader who does not
loses nothing.

## Lifting this seed

1. Read the two essays, then read three factors lessons, then read the pitch.
2. Write one paragraph of your own product documentation in that shape: trade, cost, recommendation.
3. Have someone check whether each sentence could be false. If it cannot be checked, cut it.
4. Do not import the legume titles into a project where they would need explaining.

## What it costs to host

- **More work per sentence.** A claim takes a minute; a receipt takes research.
- **You will be wrong in public.** Specific numbers age, and someone will notice. That is the deal.
- **It is not for every surface.** Release notes, API references and error messages want plainness,
  not craft. The voice is for the pages that argue.

## What it does not cover

Marketing copy, support replies, or how to write a changelog. It covers the register this site uses
when it is trying to convince someone of something checkable.
